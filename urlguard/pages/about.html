InControl

Block sneaky redirects and third-party background requests. You stay in control.

🛡 No data leaves your browser. No analytics. No accounts. Everything stays on your device.

What it catches

InControl watches three kinds of behavior that normal browsing tools ignore:

  • Redirect hijacks — you click a link to site A, it silently redirects through B, C, D before landing somewhere else. InControl surfaces the full chain.
  • Background fires — you load a page and it quietly fires requests to dozens of other domains (trackers, ad networks, fingerprinters). InControl lists them per tab so you can see exactly who your current page is talking to.
  • Ad overlays on videos — an ad is stacked on top of the video player so your click on the video opens a spam page instead. InControl removes the overlay and shows you which domains it was pointing at.
  • Popunders — a script waits for you to click anywhere, then opens an unrelated site in a new tab that steals focus. InControl cancels those opens when the click was on a video, and lists the rest so you can block them.

Video guard

Video guard runs on every page and does two things.

Overlay removal

It looks for an element that sits on top of a video player, covers most of it, and contains a link or embedded frame to a different domain than the site you are on. That combination is an ad overlay; the player's own controls, poster and play button never match. When it finds one it removes it, and if you click an overlay that slipped in between checks, the click is swallowed and the overlay is removed instead of opening the ad. Removed overlays appear in Activity as Overlay ads removed.

Popup blocking

Every window.open and every new-tab link is checked. A third-party target is cancelled when the click that triggered it landed inside a video or a video embed, when it landed on a first-party link such as a page number (a link that keeps you on the site must never open an ad tab), or when the target is on your Blocked list. Third-party popups triggered from a genuine button elsewhere are allowed, because sign-in and payment flows depend on them, but they appear in Activity under Popups opened so you can block the domain with one click. Cancelled ones show under Popups blocked.

If a site misbehaves, use Turn off for this site in the popup. Marking a domain as Allowed tells video guard to leave overlays and popups pointing at that domain alone.

Block all pop-ups to other sites

Some sites open an ad tab on any click, even on empty space. For those, turn on Block all pop-ups to other sites in the popup. While it is on for that site, every script-opened pop-up to another domain is cancelled. It is off by default and applies only to sites you switch it on for.

It does not touch the hyperlinks you click. Ordinary links, including ones that open in a new tab, are normal browser navigations and are never affected. It only cancels pop-ups a script opens with window.open, which is what these ad tabs use. A pop-up you do want (a sign-in or payment window) can be kept by marking its domain as Allowed.

How to use the popup

Click the InControl icon in the toolbar. The popup has three tabs:

Activity

Live view of what's happening on the current tab. Redirect chains and third-party domains appear as a tree. Hover any domain to Block or Allow it.

Blocked

Every domain you've chosen to block. Future requests to these domains are stopped before they load. Hover to unblock.

Allowed

Domains you've marked as trusted. InControl stops flagging these in the Activity view. Useful for CDNs or services you rely on.

How blocking actually works

When you block a domain, InControl adds a rule to Chrome's declarativeNetRequest engine. From that point on, requests to that domain are refused at the network layer — the page never sees a response. Blocking persists across browser sessions.

Block this site

The Block this site button in the popup header blocks the domain of the page you're currently on. Visiting it again will fail — useful for shady redirect targets or sites you want to permanently avoid.

Block all 3rd party domains

When a page has many third-party requests, a Block all bar appears. One click blocks every third-party domain firing on that tab. Use carefully — legitimate sites often load from CDNs that you'll want to allow again afterward.

What shows up in "Activity"

A typical entry might look like:

  • A redirect chain: news-site.com → ads.net → tracker.io → destination.com
  • A background tree: the page domain at the root, with each third-party domain hanging off as a child node
  • A blocked badge: if InControl already stopped something on this tab, a red shield summarizes the count

Permissions, and why

InControl asks for broad permissions because redirect detection requires watching all tabs, not just one. Here is what each is used for:

  • webRequest + webNavigation — observe redirects and third-party fires in real time
  • tabs — read the active tab's URL to scope the popup view to the current page
  • storage — remember your blocked and allowed lists across sessions
  • declarativeNetRequest — enforce blocks at the network layer
  • <all_urls> — required to monitor behavior on any site you visit
  • Content scripts on all pages — needed by video guard to find and remove ad overlays on video players and to cancel popunders opened from a click on a video. They read nothing else and send nothing anywhere.

⚠ Blocking breaks things. If a site stops working, check the Blocked tab — chances are a CDN or auth domain got swept in. Unblock and it'll work again.
